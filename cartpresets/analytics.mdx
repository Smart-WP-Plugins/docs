<Info>
  The Presets report is a Pro feature. On the free version there's no menu entry and no report. It's absent, not switched off.
</Info>

## Where to find it

**Analytics → Cart Presets**, beside WooCommerce's own reports.

You can also jump straight to one preset's report from the CartPresets admin. Click a preset's figure in the list's **Revenue** column, or **View in Analytics** in the editor. Either one opens the report filtered to that preset.

<Media>The Presets report with the date range picker, summary cards, chart and table</Media>

## What it shows

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Summary cards | Preset net sales, preset orders, items sold via presets, and average preset revenue per order, for the period |
| Chart | Revenue or orders over time, by day, week or month, with period comparison |
| Show | All presets, a single preset, or a comparison of the presets you choose |
| Table | One row per preset — net sales, orders, items sold, average revenue per order |
| Drill-down | The orders behind a preset — date, order number, status, customer and customer type, products, items sold, coupons, net sales and attribution |

The date-range picker, the comparison and the chart controls are all WooCommerce's own components, and the export uses WooCommerce's own CSV exporter, so the page behaves just like the reports beside it.

## Where the numbers come from

WooCommerce's own order tables, joined against the preset ID stamped on every preset order line at checkout.

Three things follow from that:

**Revenue is net of refunds.** A refund carries negative revenue and its line items carry the preset meta, so the subtraction lands on the right preset. Order counts use parent orders only, so a refunded order is one order, not two.

**A sale is whatever WooCommerce says it is.** The excluded-status list is WooCommerce's own, so this page agrees with every other page under Analytics instead of keeping its own opinion.

**A deleted preset keeps its history**, listed as *Deleted preset #N*. The money was real, and a report whose rows vanish with the preset won't match your accounts.

## What is not tracked

Nothing records link opens, add-to-carts or checkout-reached.

Following a preset link makes **zero analytics writes**. Sales enter the books when WooCommerce processes the order, on hooks that never run on your storefront. That's how the plugin keeps a near-zero storefront footprint.

If you want click-level attribution, put UTM parameters on your preset links and read them in your existing analytics. See [Preset links](/cartpresets/links/preset-links#utm-parameters).

<Note>
  If WooCommerce Analytics is disabled, there's no Presets page, and the plugin keeps no record of its own. Whether sales are tracked at all is your WooCommerce decision, and CartPresets follows it.
</Note>

## Upgrading starts with history

The report reads WooCommerce's own books rather than a record of its own, so upgrading from free doesn't start you at zero. The figures have been adding up all along; there was just nowhere to see them.

## Revenue in the listing

The preset list has a **Revenue** column, with the preset's order count under each figure. It's the same number this report shows: WooCommerce's net revenue on the order lines the preset was stamped on. It isn't an estimate from today's price, so refunds and old prices are already in it.

You can also sort the list by **Revenue**, and filter it with a Revenue condition. Both use that same figure, so a row never sorts or filters by one number while showing another.

On the free version the column is there but locked. Its header carries a Pro tag that explains it once, and each row shows a lock where the figure would be. Revenue shows as **Revenue (Pro)** in the sort and filter choices, and can't be picked.

## Reporting and purchase limits are different systems

Purchase limits never read any of this.

Limits count rows in the redemptions table, under **Settings → Usage limits → Count orders with these statuses** (`redemption_statuses`). So an on-hold order can take a slot before it counts as a sale on this page.

The two answer different questions: *may this link still be used?* versus *how did this preset do?* A bank-transfer order is a real commitment for the first question, and not yet revenue for the second.

## For developers

`cartpresets_sales_payload` runs after totals, series, rows and the drill-down are assembled. Use it to append figures of your own, or to reshape what the report receives. `cartpresets_sales_orders_cap` raises how many orders the drill-down lists.

Both fire from CartPresets for WooCommerce Pro only, and the `cartpresets/v1/sales` routes aren't registered on the free version, so a request 404s like any unknown route. Check the tier before assuming they exist. See the [hooks reference](/cartpresets/developers/hooks-reference#reporting-pro-only).
