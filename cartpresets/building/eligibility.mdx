<Info>
  Eligibility is a Pro feature. On the free version the two settings are stored but not enforced — every visitor is eligible. The sign-in switch is drawn off and locked, and the role picker does not appear at all.
</Info>

By default anyone holding the link can use it. Eligibility narrows that to signed-in customers, or to particular roles.

It lives in the editor's **Usage restrictions** card, under **Customer access**.

<Media>The Customer access block with the sign-in switch on and two roles chosen</Media>

## Two gates, and one belongs to the other

**Signed-in customers only.** A guest following the link is refused, exactly as any other refused visitor is: they are told why, and go wherever your **Unavailable links** setting sends them. Once signed in, they can follow the link again.

**Limit to roles.** Choose one or more WordPress roles. A visitor whose account holds none of them is turned away.

The role picker appears **only while the sign-in switch is on**, and turning that switch off clears the roles it held. Roles are a narrowing of "signed in", never a rule of their own — a guest holds no roles, so a role list without the switch would refuse every visitor while the screen showed no sign-in requirement at all.

<Note>
  The same rule holds below the screen: a role list stored without the switch turns the switch on when it is saved. An import or a filter cannot smuggle in a restriction the editor has no way to show you.
</Note>

<Tip>
  This is how a wholesale or staff preset stays out of public reach even though the link itself is not secret.
</Tip>

## Why only these two

Both are facts WordPress knows with certainty on every request.

Ship-to region was considered and removed. A visitor's country is frequently unknowable at the moment they click a link, and a restriction that silently holds for some visitors and not others is worse than no restriction at all — you would believe the preset was protected when it was not.

## What a rejected visitor reads

Two sentences, both store-wide, both editable under **Settings → Messages and labels**:

| Situation | Shipped wording | Notice ID |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A guest meets a signed-in-only preset | Please sign in to use this preset. | `not_signed_in` |
| An account holds none of the allowed roles | This preset is not available on your account. | `role_not_allowed` |

A guest meeting a role-restricted preset reads the **sign-in** sentence, not the account one: roles imply sign-in, and that gate is asked first. It is also the more useful message for somebody who is not signed in.

<Note>
  These used to be one message set on each preset, in a field none of the other eight refusal sentences lived beside. They are part of the wording catalogue now, so every sentence a customer can read is edited in one place.
</Note>

<Warning>
  Write them for a stranger. Anyone with the link can trigger them, so they should not confirm what the preset contains or who it is for.
</Warning>

## Where they land

Wherever every refused visitor lands: the **Unavailable links** setting, under **Settings → Cart behaviour**, overridable per preset. The cart, or a custom link of your own. See [Where customers land](/cartpresets/links/destinations).

There is **no sign-in redirect**. A guest is not sent to a login form and returned — the plugin refuses the link and says why, and where they go next is your setting rather than a special case.

External addresses work. A merchant-configured destination passes through a redirect helper that allow-lists the host you configured — WordPress's own `wp_safe_redirect()` would silently rewrite a foreign host to wp-admin, which would send a rejected customer to your dashboard login.

## Notices reach block checkouts too

WooCommerce's Cart and Checkout **blocks** on a classic theme never print the classic notice queue. A message written into the session would sit there until some classic template dumped every stale notice at once.

CartPresets prepends the pending queue to both blocks through WooCommerce's own notice templates, so your rejection message arrives whichever cart your theme uses.

<Warning>
  A custom link is the one place a message can go unseen: the page you send them to has to print WooCommerce notices, and a landing page often does not.
</Warning>

## Settings reference

| Setting | Level | Key | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Signed-in customers only | Preset | `logged_in` | Off |
| Limit to roles | Preset | `roles` | Empty (any signed-in customer) |
| Unavailable links | Store | `refused_action` | `cart` |

## For developers

Eligibility is decided through `cartpresets_eligibility`, seeded `allowed => true`. Every verdict comes from a listener, including the plugin's own — so you can add a rule of your own (a membership check, a regional policy) at a later priority and have the last word. See the [hooks reference](/cartpresets/developers/hooks-reference#availability-and-eligibility).
