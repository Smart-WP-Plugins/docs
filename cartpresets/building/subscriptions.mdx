Presets work with WooCommerce Subscriptions products on both versions. A preset discount on a subscription applies to every payment for as long as the subscription runs. Pro lets you stop it after a set number of payments, and waive the sign-up fee.

## The question a subscription raises

A 20% discount on a one-off product is clear enough. On a subscription it could cover the first payment only, or every renewal for the life of the subscription.

CartPresets discounts every payment unless you set a limit. That's what a preset price on a subscription usually means, and it matches the cart line: "$5.00 every month" is exactly true.

## What the free version does

It discounts every payment and charges the sign-up fee, whatever the item's stored settings say.

The editor says so where you set the discount. An item's **Subscription** panel shows **Discounted payments** locked at **Every payment**, with the note *The discount applies to every payment for as long as the subscription runs.* **Waive the sign-up fee** is shown off and locked, because the fee is always charged here. The item's line reads "every payment" too.

<Note>
  Both versions store the two settings. A limit or a waiver set on Pro is kept on the free version but not applied. When you upgrade, your existing answers apply again with nothing to set up.
</Note>

## Choosing on Pro<Pro />

Each subscription item's **Subscription** panel has a **Discounted payments** field and a **Waive the sign-up fee** switch.

<Media>A subscription item expanded, showing the Discounted payments field and the waive-fee switch</Media>

### Discounted payments

| Value | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Empty (every payment) | The discounted price applies for the life of the subscription. The cart line's "every month" price string is exactly true |
| A number | The discount covers that many payments, then renewals bill the product's own price |

Payments are counted the way WooCommerce Subscriptions counts them, including the first one. "3" means the initial order and two renewals. The note under the field spells out the result in your numbers, for example *The first 3 payments are discounted, then $9.99 resumes.*

<Tip>
  This is what an introductory offer needs: three months at a discount, then the normal price, with nobody having to step in.
</Tip>

### When the discount runs out

The line is set back to the product's own live price. It has to be reset, because the discounted price has already been written onto the line.

The limit and the price to return to are written onto the subscription at checkout. Editing the preset later doesn't change what an existing subscriber agreed to.

The price renewals return to is the same baseline the editor quoted. If the item uses **Use the regular price instead of the sale price**, renewals bill the regular price even if a sale is running at the time, so a sale can't stamp a lower renewal price than you set.

<Warning>
  A limited discount changes the subscription's amount partway through, so the payment gateway has to support that. When the cart holds a limited discount, checkout hides subscription gateways that can't change a subscription's amount. WooCommerce Subscriptions does the same for its own limited coupons.
</Warning>

### When a customer resubscribes

The count carries over. A customer who cancels and resubscribes from **My account** picks up where they left off, on the terms their first subscription had.

- Two of three discounted payments used: the resubscribe's first payment is the third, still discounted. Renewals after it bill the full price.
- All three used: the new subscription is at the full price from the start.

So cancelling and resubscribing can't restart an introductory offer.

### Waive the sign-up fee

Zeroes the product's sign-up fee for that line only. The mark travels in memory and is never saved to the product, so other customers buying the same product outside this preset still pay the fee.

## Renewal protections

Three protections apply automatically:

- Renewal orders never use up a purchase-limit slot. They inherit the preset's meta from the subscription, and without the guard one subscriber's renewals would use up a capped preset on their own.
- A renewal paid through the cart is never repriced. The subscription decides what a renewal costs, not the preset.
- Products that can only be bought as a subscription plan (All Products for Subscriptions' forced mode) are refused as preset items, and the product picker leaves them out. A preset adds by product ID with no plan, and when two pricing engines both price one line, one of the prices shown is wrong.

## A known limitation

Where a discount covers a fixed number of payments, WooCommerce's cart line still shows a recurring price string such as "36.00 every month", even though the discount won't last for the whole subscription.

The string comes from Subscriptions' own templates, built before the preset's terms are worked out. The order confirmation and the subscription record are correct. Say so in your offer copy if the difference matters to your customers.

## Settings reference

| Setting | Level | Key | Default | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discounted payments | Item | `sub_payments` | `0` (every payment) | Stored on both, honoured on Pro |
| Waive the sign-up fee | Item | `sub_waive_fee` | Off | Stored on both, honoured on Pro |

## For developers

Both are resolved through `cartpresets_item_subscription_terms`, returning `[ 'payments' => int, 'waive_fee' => bool ]`, where `payments` counts the payments the discount covers, including the one being made, and `0` means every payment for the life of the subscription.

It's seeded with the free version's reading: `payments => 0` (every payment) and the fee charged. A listener that returns no `payments` key keeps the seed, so an integration that only answers `waive_fee` can't cut the discount short by accident. A `payments` value goes through `absint()`. See the [hooks reference](/cartpresets/developers/hooks-reference#pricing).
