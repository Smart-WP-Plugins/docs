Presets work with WooCommerce Subscriptions products on both versions. What differs is how far a discount reaches.

## The question a subscription raises

A 20% discount on a one-off product is clear enough. On a subscription it isn't: does it buy the **first payment**, or **every renewal for the life of the subscription**?

Get that wrong in the generous direction and you find out a year later, from a subscription that's still renewing, that a link you made once granted a permanent discount.

## What the free version does

**The first payment, with the sign-up fee charged.** Always, whatever the item's stored settings say.

That's the cautious reading, and it's what shared code does with nothing attached. It leans one way on purpose: if you can't see the controls, a permanent discount mustn't take you by surprise.

<Note>
  The two settings are **stored** on both versions. Upgrading turns your existing answers on rather than asking you to set them up again.
</Note>

## Choosing on Pro<Pro />

Each subscription item in the editor gets a **Payments** field and a **Waive the sign-up fee** switch.

<Media>A subscription item expanded, showing the Payments field and the waive-fee switch</Media>

### Payments

| Value | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Empty (every payment) | The discounted price applies for the life of the subscription. The cart line's "every month" price string is exactly true |
| A number | The discount covers that many payments, then renewals bill the product's own price |

Payments are counted the way WooCommerce Subscriptions counts them, **including the first one**. "Three payments" means the initial order and two renewals.

<Warning>
  **An empty Payments field means every payment, so check it before you upgrade.** On the free version a subscription item is always discounted for the first payment only. On Pro the field is read, and left empty it means the life of the subscription. An item you never filled in therefore discounts one payment on free and every payment on Pro. If you built subscription items before upgrading, set a number on each one first.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  This is what an introductory offer actually needs: three months at a discount, then the normal price, with nobody having to step in.
</Tip>

### When the discount runs out

The line is actively restored to the product's own live price. Restored, not just skipped, because the first pass has already stamped the discounted price onto the line.

The price renewals return to is the same baseline the editor quoted. If the item uses **Use the regular price instead of the sale price**, renewals bill the regular price even if a sale is running at the time, so a sale can't stamp a lower renewal price than you set.

### Waive the sign-up fee

Zeroes the product's sign-up fee for that line only. The mark travels in memory and is never saved to the product, so other customers buying the same product outside this preset still pay the fee.

On the free version the switch shows **off**, because the fee is always charged there. Off is the honest reading of what will happen.

## Renewal protections

Three things hold automatically:

- **Renewal orders never consume a purchase-limit slot.** They inherit the preset's meta from the subscription, and without the guard one subscriber's renewals would use up a capped preset on their own.
- **A renewal paid through the cart is never repriced.** The subscription decides what a renewal costs, not the preset.
- **Products that can only be bought as a subscription plan** — All Products for Subscriptions' forced mode — are refused as preset items. A preset adds by product ID with no plan, and when two pricing engines fight over one line, one of the two prices shown is wrong.

## A known limitation

Where a discount covers a fixed number of payments, WooCommerce's cart line still shows a recurring price string — "36.00 every month" — that the discount won't hold for the whole life of the subscription.

The string comes from Subscriptions' own templates, built before the preset's terms are worked out. The order confirmation and the subscription record are correct. Say so in your offer copy if the difference matters to your customers.

## Settings reference

| Setting | Level | Key | Default | Tier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Payments | Item | `sub_payments` | `0` (every payment) | Stored on both, honoured on Pro |
| Waive the sign-up fee | Item | `sub_waive_fee` | Off | Stored on both, honoured on Pro |

## For developers

Both are resolved through `cartpresets_item_subscription_terms`, returning `[ 'payments' => int, 'waive_fee' => bool ]`, where `payments` counts the payments the discount covers **including the one being made now** and `0` means every payment for the life of the subscription.

Seeded cautiously — one payment, fee charged — and clamped for safety. An array with no `payments` key reads as `1` rather than `0`, and the value goes through `absint()`, so no listener can grant an endless discount by accident. See the [hooks reference](/cartpresets/developers/hooks-reference#pricing).
