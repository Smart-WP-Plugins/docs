<Info>
  Scheduling is a Pro feature. On the free version a preset goes live the moment you save it with the status switch on, and never ends on its own. The date controls are visible but locked, and a date already stored still holds a preset back or ends it.
</Info>

A schedule is two dates. Once the second one has passed, a customer meets the same answer every other refused link gets.

<Media>The Preset details card with the Date field, and the Usage restrictions card with Preset ends on</Media>

## The start date

Choose a **Date** in the future, in the **Preset details** card, and the preset reads as **Scheduled** until that day arrives. Anyone following the link before then gets the _This preset is not available yet._ notice.

A preset with no date, or one whose date has passed, is live as soon as its status switch is on.

Dates are whole days, on your store's clock. A start date opens at the first moment of its day. An end date closes at the last, so the preset stays live all through its end day.

<Tip>
  This is how a campaign link gets built and approved weeks early, then opens on its own on launch day with nobody awake for it.
</Tip>

## The end date

Set **Preset ends on**, in the **Usage restrictions** card, and the preset stops selling once that day is over. It reads as **Expired** afterwards.

It's the third field in the **Purchase limits** row, beside the two caps. Between them the three answer one question: when does this link stop selling, and after how many orders.

A seasonal offer closes itself instead of relying on someone remembering.

A cart filled before the deadline can't check out after it. Checkout asks the same question the link does.

## What happens when it ends

An ended preset isn't a broken link, and it isn't a special case either. Where the customer lands is the store's **Unavailable links** setting, which answers for every way a link can be refused: expired, sold out, switched off, used up, restricted, or pointing at no preset at all.

| Level | Where | Choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store | **Settings → Cart behaviour → Unavailable links** | **Cart** (the default) or **Custom URL**, plus **Show an error message** |
| Preset<Pro /> | The editor's **Cart behaviour** card, under **Unavailable links** | **Use the Settings default**, or its own answer and its own **Custom link** |

The destination and the message are separate questions. **Show an error message** defaults to on, and that's on purpose. Picture a customer who follows a link they were given, arrives holding nothing new, and is told nothing. As far as they can tell, your store is broken.

Turn it off once you've got somewhere better to send them, usually a custom link to a landing page that explains the offer has closed and shows what else you sell.

<Warning>
  A custom page only shows the reason if it prints WooCommerce notices. Most themes do on WooCommerce pages; a landing page often doesn't. The cart always does.
</Warning>

Full detail: [Where customers land](/cartpresets/links/destinations).

<Media>The editor's Cart behaviour card showing Unavailable links and its custom link field</Media>

## Putting an ended preset back on sale

Clearing the end date brings it back. Two ways in:

- The **Preset ends on** field in the editor — clear it and save.
- **Clear end date** in the listing's row menu, which only appears on presets that have one.

<Note>
  Clearing an end date is gated exactly like setting one. On the free version a preset that ended stays ended. Moving to the free version must never put a link back on sale that your own schedule had stopped.
</Note>

## Status is derived, never stored

The status badge is worked out from the status switch, the dates and the purchase limit every time it's read. Nothing runs on a cron to flip it, and nothing can go stale.

| Badge | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Active | Switch is on, the start date has passed, the end date has not, and the limit is not used up |
| Scheduled | Switch is on, and the start date is in the future |
| Expired | Switch is on, and the end date has passed |
| Sold out | Switch is on, and the purchase limit is used up |
| Inactive | Switch is off — whatever the dates and limits say |

The switch outranks everything, and an expired date outranks a full limit, because you can raise a limit but you can't argue with a date.

That's why a preset can go live on a site nobody visited overnight. There's nothing to run.

## Settings reference

| Setting | Level | Key | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Date (publish) | Preset | `created_at` | Now |
| Preset ends on | Preset | `end_at` | Empty |
| Unavailable links | Store | `refused_action` | `cart` |
| Custom link | Store | `refused_url` | Empty |
| Show an error message | Store | `refused_notice` | On |
| Unavailable links | Preset | `refused_action` | `global` |
| Custom link | Preset | `refused_url` | Empty |
| Show an error message | Preset | `refused_notice` | On |

The store-wide answer is editable on both versions. Setting one on a single preset needs Pro, and one already stored is honoured on either.
