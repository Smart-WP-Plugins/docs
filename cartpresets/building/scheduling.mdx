<Info>
  Scheduling is a Pro feature. On the free version, **Starts on** and **Preset ends on** are shown locked with a Pro tag. A new preset goes live the moment you save it with its link enabled, and never ends on its own. A date already stored still holds a preset back or ends it.
</Info>

A schedule is two moments: when the preset starts selling, and when it stops. Each one is a date **and** a time, on your store's clock. Once the end has passed, a customer meets the same answer every other refused link gets.

<Media>The Preset details card with the Starts on field, and the Usage restrictions card with Preset ends on</Media>

## Entering a date and time

Click a date field to open a calendar. Pick a day, set the time under the month, then click **Done**. **Clear** empties the field.

You can also type straight into the field, in the form *Nov 19, 2026, 9:00 AM*. Anything else is flagged with an example of the format.

The note under each field says what your answer means, in words, for example *Ends on Jan 18, 2027 at 11:59 PM, store time.*

## The start

Set **Starts on**, in the **Preset details** card, to a moment in the future and the preset reads as **Scheduled** until then. Anyone following the link before that gets the _This preset is not available yet._ notice.

Leave it empty and the preset starts as soon as it's active. The empty field says so: *Starts when active*. When you save, an empty **Starts on** is filled in with the moment of the save. A start that's already passed works the same way.

<Tip>
  This is how a campaign link gets built and approved weeks early, then opens on its own on launch day with nobody awake for it.
</Tip>

## The end

Set **Preset ends on**, in the **Usage restrictions** card, and the preset stops selling at that moment. It reads as **Expired** afterwards. Leave it empty and the preset runs until you switch it off.

It's the third field under **Purchase limits**, beside **Maximum orders** and **Maximum orders per customer**. Between them the three answer one question: when does this link stop selling, and after how many orders.

A seasonal offer closes itself instead of relying on someone remembering.

A cart filled before the deadline can't check out after it. Checkout asks the same question the link does.

<Note>
  The end must come after the start. If it doesn't, the health checks warn you and the save is refused with *The end date is before the publish date.* on **Preset ends on**. Both moments are compared to the minute, so a preset can start and end on the same day.
</Note>

## What happens when it ends

An ended preset isn't a broken link, and it isn't a special case either. Where the customer lands is the store's **Unavailable links** setting, which answers for every way a link can be refused: expired, sold out, switched off, used up, restricted, or pointing at no preset at all.

| Level | Where | Choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Store | **Settings → Cart behaviour → Unavailable links** | **Cart** (the default) or **Custom URL**, plus **Show an error message** |
| Preset<Pro /> | The editor's **Cart behaviour** card, under **Unavailable links** | **Store default**, or **Override** with its own **Cart** or **Custom URL** |

The destination and the message are separate questions. **Show an error message** defaults to on, and that's on purpose. Picture a customer who follows a link they were given, arrives holding nothing new, and is told nothing. As far as they can tell, your store is broken.

Turn it off once you've got somewhere better to send them, usually a custom URL for a landing page that explains the offer has closed and shows what else you sell.

<Warning>
  A custom page only shows the reason if it prints WooCommerce notices. Most themes do on WooCommerce pages; a landing page often doesn't. The cart always does.
</Warning>

Full detail: [Where customers land](/cartpresets/links/destinations).

## Putting an ended preset back on sale

Clearing the end date brings it back. Two ways in:

- The **Preset ends on** field in the editor — clear it and save.
- **Clear end date** in the listing's row menu, which only appears on presets that have one.

<Note>
  Clearing an end date is gated exactly like setting one. On the free version **Clear end date** is locked with a Pro tag, and a preset that ended stays ended. Moving to the free version must never put a link back on sale that your own schedule had stopped.
</Note>

## Status is derived, never stored

The status badge is worked out from **Link enabled**, the two dates and the purchase limit every time it's read. Nothing runs on a cron to flip it, and nothing can go stale.

| Badge | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Active | Link enabled, the start has passed, the end has not, and the limit is not used up |
| Scheduled | Link enabled, and the start is in the future |
| Expired | Link enabled, and the end has passed |
| Sold out | Link enabled, and the purchase limit is used up |
| Inactive | Link switched off — whatever the dates and limits say |

The switch outranks everything, and an expired date outranks a full limit, because you can raise a limit but you can't argue with a date.

That's why a preset can go live on a site nobody visited overnight. There's nothing to run.

## Settings reference

| Setting | Level | Key | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starts on | Preset | `created_at` | The moment of the save, if left empty |
| Preset ends on | Preset | `end_at` | Empty |
| Unavailable links | Store | `refused_action` | `cart` |
| Custom URL | Store | `refused_url` | Empty |
| Show an error message | Store | `refused_notice` | On |
| Unavailable links | Preset | `refused_action` | `global` (the store default) |
| Custom URL | Preset | `refused_url` | Empty |

Both dates are stored as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS` in your store's time zone. A date stored without a time starts at the first moment of its day, or ends at the last.

The store-wide answer is editable on both versions. Setting one on a single preset needs Pro, and one already stored is honoured on either.
